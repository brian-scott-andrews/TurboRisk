Rules

TurboRisk is a turn-based game for 2 to 10 players (the original board game was up to 6 players) and is played on a stylized political map of the Earth, divided into 42 territories, which are grouped into 6 continents.

Here follow the general rules of the game. A large amount of customization is possible through the Options > Rules menu item.

Goal

Object of the game is to conquer the world by occupying every territory on the board, thus eliminating all of the other players.

Board setup

Each player receives a number of armies for initial deployment. This number depends on the number of players.

TurboRisk supports two alternative methods to assign territories to the players:

  1. Players take turns claiming territories by placing one army on an unoccupied territory.
  2. All the territories are assigned at random.

After all territories have been assigned, players take turns placing their remaining armies on their territories.

Playing

Each player's turn is made by 3 phases:

1. Getting and placing new armies

At the start of each turn, the player receives some reinforcements to add to any of its own territories.
  • The player receives 1 army every 3 territories he owns (rounded to the lowest integer).
  • The player receives bonus armies for occupying all of a continent.
Regardless of how many territories the player owns, he always receives a minimum of three armies per turn.

The player may also receive armies if he turns in a set of cards.

There are four kind of cards: artillery, infantry, cavalry and joker. In the traditional board game the deck is made of 14 cards per kind of artillery, infantry and cavalry, plus 2 jokers, for a total of 44 cards.

TurboRisk does not use a "true" deck with 44 cards, but simulates the process of drawing cards with a stochastic routine. This means that the probability to draw, for example, a joker is 2/44, but it may actually happen that there are more than two jokers in players' hands.

A valid set of cards consists of one of the following:

  • Three cards of the same kind.
  • Three cards one of each kind.
The joker can be used in place of any other of the three kinds.

Once a player trades in a valid set of card, he gets some armies to reinforce his own territories. This value depends on how TurboRisk has been configured. There are two options:

  • Combination: the value of the set is based on the particular combination of cards.
  • Progression: the value comes from the number of trades the player has done up to the moment.

Both of these methods allow for further customization.

The player has now to place the armies he received from the territory count and cards trade in. He can place them in one or more of the territories he owns.

2. Attacking

Attacks can only occur between two adjacent territories, one owned by the attacking player, and the other owned by a different player. In the traditional board game the outcomes of the battle is decided by rolling dice. In TurboRisk this is process is simulated through a random numbers generator.

Each roll of the dice is considered an individual attack, and the attacking player may attack any number of territories any number of times (including switching back and forth between targets).

In the board game the attacking player may roll one, two, or three dice (but at most one less than the number of armies in the attacking territory). The defending player rolls either one or two (but at most the number of armies in the defending territory).
In TurboRisk the player cannot choose the number of dice to roll: the program always rolls the maximum number of dice allowed for that situation. This may seem a limitation, but it's a choice that both maximizes each player's possibilities to win and speeds up the game.

The result of the attack is obtained comparing the dice:

  • The attacker's highest die is compared against the defender's highest die.
  • The attacker's second-highest die is compared against the defender's second-highest die (if each rolled at least two dice).
  • Any other dice are disregarded.
Higher numbers win, with the defender winning the tie. For each loss, the loser removes one army from his territory.

If an attack successfully eliminates the last defending army in a territory, the territory itself gets conquered by the attacking player, who must move at least the number of dice he attacked with into it. There is no limit to the total number of additional armies that he may move, provided that at least one army remains in the attacking territory.

If an attacking player conquers a player's last territory, thus eliminating him from the game, the attacker is awarded all of the defender's cards. If the conquering player now has got a valid set of cards, he can trade them in and receive new armies. The gained armies are placed immediately.

3. Fortifying

When finished attacking, a player has the option to move any number of armies from one of his territories into an adjacent territory that he already owns. The player must still leave at least one unit in each territory.

In the board game Fortification usually ends the turn. In TurboRisk this rule can be modified so as to allow for Fortification at any time during a turn. However, only one fortification per turn is allowed.

At the end of the turn, if the player has conquered at least one territory, he receives a new card. He must wait until his next turn to trade in any cards.

Play then proceeds to the next player.
