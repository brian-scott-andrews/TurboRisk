TRP Bot Strategies

The following TRP scripts are authored by Brian S. Andrews and were created with AI assistance. Each script includes this author credit, an AI disclaimer, and a description in its source header. These strategies are heuristics, not guarantees; actual behavior may differ from the description. The script source is the definitive reference for behavior.

Strategies

  • Grudge (grudge.trp) tracks who captures its former territories and favors attacking the opponent responsible for the most losses. Human players win ties. If the target is unavailable, it attacks other opponents only at a force ratio of at least 1.5. TRPs cannot observe failed attacks, so captured territories are used to measure hostile actions.
  • OddsMaker (oddsmaker.trp) estimates attack quality from force ratios and expected exchanges, avoids low-odds attacks, and concentrates armies at its best attack front.
  • BonusSaboteur (bonussaboteur.trp) seeks a cheap conquest that breaks an opponent's continent bonus; otherwise it targets a weak border.
  • CardCollector (cardcollector.trp) seeks one low-risk conquest per turn for a card, then stops attacking for that turn.
  • Chokepoint (chokepoint.trp) values connected territories and continent entry points, seeking control of routes between regions.
  • Quartermaster (quartermaster.trp) concentrates forces at a favorable front and moves its largest interior reserve toward a front.
  • Turtle (turtle.trp) favors survival and a compact frontier, attacking only at a decisive advantage or for a low-risk card.
  • Kingmaker (kingmaker.trp) scores opponents by military and strategic strength, then directs attacks toward the leader.
  • Comeback (comeback.trp) targets weak opponents and low-risk territories when behind; when ahead, it raises its attack threshold and invests in continent bonuses.
  • Opportunist (opportunist.trp) re-evaluates attack opportunities using force ratio, eliminations, continent bonuses, and strategic access.
  • Washington (washington.trp) uses a Fabian-inspired approach: preserves forces, avoids unnecessary battles, reinforces exposed fronts, and advances from favorable positions.
  • Doomstack (doomstack.trp) builds one concentrated attack army, takes inexpensive conquests for cards, and plans budgeted eliminations of weak card-holding players. In games of four or fewer players it also targets and defends continents. To break stalemates it attacks giant stacks held on one or two territories. Inspired by brainless IV (Mattias Beimers) and Vexer's elimination planning; independently implemented.
  • Actuary (actuary.trp) prices every decision with exact dice odds. Each attack roll is re-evaluated as an expected value, so a battle whose odds turn bad is abandoned. It plans chained eliminations of weak card-holding players, reinforces continent entries that are likely to fall, and builds one stack aimed at the most achievable continent. Draws on common Risk strategy-guide advice and Doomstack's stack ideas.
